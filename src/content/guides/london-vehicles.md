## Overview

UK diplomatic vehicle registration is managed jointly by the FCDO Protocol Directorate and the DVLA. The plate format is distinctive (numeric, not alphabetic as in France), and the exemptions, while real, do not cover everything that diplomats often assume they cover. The Congestion Charge is the most significant practical friction point for diplomatic families based or working in central London.

---

## Do you need a car in London?

London has one of the most extensive public transport networks in Europe. The Underground, Overground, Elizabeth line, buses and National Rail services connect virtually every part of Greater London. For postings based in the SW1, W8 or WC2 diplomatic belt, daily commuting by public transport is practical and generally faster than driving in central London.

The case for a car strengthens for families with children at schools in the Surrey corridor (TASIS in Thorpe, ACS Cobham, ACS Egham), for staff based in outer London boroughs, or for those with regular travel requirements outside London. The Congestion Charge and parking costs make car ownership in central London expensive regardless of diplomatic status.

## The legal framework

The Diplomatic Privileges Act 1964 and the Vienna Conventions govern the general exemptions applicable to diplomatic vehicles. Specific vehicle registration provisions are administered under the Vehicle Excise and Registration Act 1994 and related DVLA regulations.

The two bodies that jointly manage the process are:

- **FCDO Protocol Directorate**: verifies diplomatic accreditation and liaises with the DVLA on eligibility
- **DVLA Specialist Registrations team**: issues the plates once accreditation is confirmed. Guidance document **INF267**, produced by the DVLA for accredited officials, sets out the process in detail. It is available from the DVLA or via your mission.

---

## Plate format

UK diplomatic plates have been issued since 1979. They have a **white background with black characters** and follow the format:

**[3 digits] [letter] [3 digits]**

For example: `270 D 101`

The letter in the middle indicates the category:
- **D**: diplomatic agents holding full diplomatic immunity under the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations
- **X**: administrative and technical staff of diplomatic missions and international organisations, and non-diplomatic accredited personnel

The first group of three digits identifies the sending country or international organisation. The second group is a serial number; the sequence starting at 101 is for diplomatic agents, 400+ for non-diplomatic international organisation staff, and 700+ for consular and other non-diplomatic categories.

Some missions also hold historic "flag plates" from earlier British registration systems (for example, CAN 1 for Canada). These predate the 1979 system and are retained by specific missions.

Honorary consuls are **not** entitled to diplomatic vehicle registrations under UK law.

## The registration procedure

All applications pass through your mission. The mission confirms your accreditation with the FCDO Protocol Directorate, which then liaises with the DVLA Specialist Registrations team for plate issuance.

**Documents typically required (confirm with your mission):**
- Evidence of diplomatic accreditation confirmed by the Protocol Directorate
- Vehicle registration details and certificate of conformity
- Proof of UK motor insurance

The DVLA issues a special registration document alongside the plates. Retain this document: it is evidence of the diplomatic exemptions attached to the vehicle.

## Exemptions

Vehicles registered on diplomatic plates are exempt from:

- **Vehicle Excise Duty (VED)**: road tax does not apply
- **MOT testing**: diplomatically-registered vehicles are not subject to the mandatory MOT inspection
- Some categories of parking enforcement, depending on the nature of the sanction and the mission's specific position

## The Congestion Charge

The London Congestion Charge is a daily charge applied to most vehicles entering the Congestion Charge Zone in central London. The UK government's position, confirmed repeatedly by the FCDO in statements to Parliament, is that **the Congestion Charge is not a tax but a charge for a service, and diplomatic missions are therefore liable to pay it**.

As of September 2025, foreign embassies collectively owed approximately GBP 164.6 million in unpaid Congestion Charge since the scheme launched in February 2003. The FCDO wrote to all missions in April 2025 pressing for payment. However, Transport for London cannot sue a diplomatic mission to recover unpaid charges, meaning enforcement is effectively limited to diplomatic pressure.

The practical situation: roughly two-thirds of embassies pay the Congestion Charge voluntarily. The remainder do not. The FCDO's formal position is that missions are liable. Whether a mission instructs its staff to pay or not is a decision made at mission level.

For individual diplomatic staff: the Congestion Charge applies to any vehicle entering the zone during charging hours (Monday to Friday, 07:00-18:00; weekends, 12:00-18:00 in 2026; confirm current hours with TfL as they are subject to revision). If your mission does not cover this charge, budget for it accordingly if you drive regularly in central London.

Current Congestion Charge information: [Transport for London: Congestion Charge](https://tfl.gov.uk/modes/driving/congestion-charge)

## Parking

Diplomatic plates do not confer automatic parking exemptions across London. The FCDO has explicitly stated that it considers diplomatic missions liable for Penalty Charge Notices (PCNs) issued by local authorities for parking infringements. As of May 2025, outstanding PCN debt notified to the FCDO by local authorities stood at approximately GBP 1.36 million.

In practice, parking enforcement at the immediate vicinity of diplomatic premises is often managed by arrangement with the relevant London borough. For private vehicle parking across the city, standard rules apply.

---

## Insurance

All vehicles circulating on UK roads, including those on diplomatic plates, must carry at minimum third-party liability insurance under the Road Traffic Act 1988. Diplomatic immunity does not exempt a vehicle from the insurance requirement. Several specialist insurers provide policies for diplomatically-registered vehicles; your mission's administrative office will know which providers are commonly used.

---

## End of posting

When diplomatic status ends, plates must be surrendered to the DVLA. Coordinate this with your mission's administrative office well before your departure date.
