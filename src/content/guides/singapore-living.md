## Overview

Singapore is a small, highly efficient city-state of approximately 734 square kilometres with no rural hinterland. It hosts 73 resident embassies and high commissions, making it the principal bilateral diplomatic hub in Southeast Asia as well as a major regional base for international organisations. Infrastructure is reliable, public transport is extensive, and the administrative environment for diplomatic staff is generally straightforward. The cost of living, particularly housing and schooling, is among the highest in Asia.

---

## The legal framework

The privileges and immunities of diplomatic and consular staff in Singapore are governed by the [Diplomatic and Consular Relations Act 2005 (DCRA 2005)](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/DCRA2005), which gives effect in Singapore law to the [Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961)](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/conventions/9_1_1961.pdf) and the [Vienna Convention on Consular Relations (1963)](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/conventions/9_2_1963.pdf).

The body responsible for accrediting diplomatic and consular personnel and managing privileges and immunities in Singapore is the **Protocol Directorate** of the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MFA)](https://www.mfa.gov.sg). The MFA Protocol Directorate facilitates state formalities, manages diplomatic privileges and immunities, and organises accreditation procedures for foreign missions. Contact for protocol matters: [mfa.gov.sg](https://www.mfa.gov.sg).

The MFA publishes a [Diplomatic and Consular List](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/262/3422801a-41e6-4a10-bf7a-88b69f5f9f18/Dipconopen.pdf) updated periodically by the Protocol Directorate.

## Diplomatic status and identity documentation

On arrival, your mission notifies the MFA of your presence and function. The Immigration and Checkpoints Authority (ICA) issues a **Diplomatic Pass** (DP) to accredited diplomatic personnel, in coordination with MFA. This is your primary identity and residence document in Singapore. Family members receive corresponding passes.

Singapore does not issue a card equivalent to the French titre de séjour spécial or Swiss legitimation card with a colour-coded privilege indicator. The Diplomatic Pass confirms your accredited status, and the scope of privileges derives from the DCRA 2005 and your accreditation category.

## Housing

Singapore's diplomatic community is concentrated in two broad areas:

**Tanglin / Holland Road / Napier Road**: the traditional diplomatic enclave, home to many bilateral embassies along Tanglin Road, Holland Road and Nassim Road. Residential properties here tend to be large detached houses or landed properties commanding premium rents (SGD 8,000-20,000+ per month for family-sized landed property). This cluster is convenient for the Tanglin diplomatic community and for families with children at Tanglin Trust School.

**Buona Vista / Holland Village / West Coast / Clementi**: popular with families whose children attend UWCSEA Dover campus or Tanglin Trust School. Housing is a mix of condominiums and landed properties; rents for a 3-4 bedroom condominium typically range from SGD 5,000 to SGD 12,000 per month.

**Woodlands / Upper Thomson / Sembawang**: north Singapore, popular with families whose children attend Singapore American School. Longer commutes to the diplomatic zone but generally lower rents.

**Orchard Road / River Valley / Novena**: central, walkable to embassies and the CBD. Mix of apartments; convenient but very expensive.

Housing in Singapore is scarce and in persistent demand from a large expatriate community. Begin searching well before your arrival date. Your mission's administrative office typically has established relationships with agencies experienced in placing diplomatic families.

## Healthcare

Singapore has one of the best healthcare systems in Asia, with both an excellent public hospital network and a well-developed private sector. Diplomatic and international staff typically use private facilities. Key private hospitals include Gleneagles Hospital, Mount Elizabeth Novena and Raffles Hospital. The standard of care is high and most medical staff speak English.

Most diplomatic missions arrange private health insurance for their staff. Confirm your coverage position with your mission or organisation before arrival. Do not assume access to the public healthcare system at subsidised rates; this is not automatically available to diplomatic pass holders.

## Cost of living

Singapore is consistently ranked among the most expensive cities in Asia. Key cost drivers for diplomatic families:

- Housing: SGD 5,000-20,000+ per month depending on location and property type
- International schooling: SGD 30,000-58,000+ per year per child (see the companion guide, SG·SCH·01)
- Private healthcare: considerable if not fully covered by mission insurance
- Groceries and dining: mid-to-high cost; hawker centres provide affordable options

Singapore has a 9% Goods and Services Tax (GST) applied to most goods and services, including international school fees. Diplomatic staff are entitled to certain tax exemptions through the MFA; these are managed through your mission's administrative office.

## Getting around

Singapore's Mass Rapid Transit (MRT) network covers all major residential and commercial areas of the island. Buses complement the MRT comprehensively. An EZ-Link card or contactless payment provides access to all public transport. Travel times by MRT from the main residential areas to Tanglin (diplomatic zone) range from 10-30 minutes, depending on origin. Taxis and ride-hailing (Grab is dominant) are widely available and generally affordable by the standards of comparable cities.
