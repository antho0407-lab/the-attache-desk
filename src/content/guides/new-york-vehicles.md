## Overview

Vehicle registration for diplomatic and consular staff in the United States is managed **federally** by the Office of Foreign Missions (OFM), regardless of which state a vehicle is registered in. In New York this means all process passes through the OFM New York regional office at 799 United Nations Plaza. One significant feature of New York's vehicle situation that distinguishes it from London: under a formal OFM determination issued in December 2024, foreign missions and international organisations are **exempt** from the NYC Congestion Relief Zone toll.

---

## Do you need a car in New York?

New York City has the most extensive public transport network in the United States. The subway runs 24 hours a day across all five boroughs. Buses, the Staten Island Ferry, the Long Island Rail Road and Metro-North commuter rail extend connectivity to the outer boroughs and the metropolitan area. For postings based in Manhattan, particularly in the UN corridor between 42nd and 68th Streets, a car is not a practical necessity for daily commuting.

The case for a car strengthens for families with children at schools in Westchester or Long Island (some international schools are suburban), for staff with regular travel to New Jersey, Connecticut or upstate New York, or for those who live in the outer boroughs. Parking in Manhattan is among the most expensive in the world. Factor this in before deciding to maintain a vehicle.

## The federal plate system

Unlike France, Switzerland, Belgium or the UK, where vehicle registration for diplomats is managed locally or bilaterally, the United States operates a **single federal system** administered by the OFM's Diplomatic Motor Vehicles (DMV) Office.

OFM diplomatic plates were introduced nationally in 1984. The current plate design, introduced in 2007, features a red, white and blue design with the State Department seal, "DIPLOMAT" at the top, "D" at the left, and "ISSUED BY THE UNITED STATES DEPARTMENT OF STATE" at the bottom.

The plate number consists of a **two-letter code** (identifying the mission or organisation) and a **three-digit serial number**. For example: "CY 101" for a Chinese diplomat. The two-letter codes are assigned by OFM and deliberately bear no similarity to the name of the country. A separate series with a "C" designation covers consular vehicles; an "S" series covers other staff categories.

## Insurance: mandatory and enforced

The OFM's vehicle programme includes an active insurance compliance mandate under the [Foreign Missions Act](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title22-section4301&num=0&edition=prelim). All vehicles registered with OFM plates must carry liability insurance. This is not optional and OFM enforces it: missions and individuals with lapsed coverage are notified directly and access to OFM vehicle services can be suspended.

OFM has periodically published updates to insurance requirements; the current policy is set out in [Diplomatic Note 24-1158 (June 2024)](https://www.state.gov/circular-notes-and-notices-diplomatic-motor-vehicles/) for international organisations and in prior notes for bilateral missions. Confirm the current requirements with your mission's administrative office.

## Driver's licences

Since August 2022, the OFM requires a valid driver's licence to register or renew a vehicle. OFM issues driver's licences to eligible mission members, or accepts national driver's licences from certain countries. The current policy and eligibility requirements are in [Diplomatic Note 22-2002](https://www.state.gov/circular-notes-and-notices-diplomatic-motor-vehicles/).

Updated guidance on driver's licence procedures, including vision exam requirements and non-driver ID requests, is issued periodically by OFM; see the [OFM Diplomatic Motor Vehicles page](https://www.state.gov/circular-notes-and-notices-diplomatic-motor-vehicles/) for the latest notices.

## The NYC Congestion Relief Zone: exempt

New York City's Congestion Relief Zone toll (commonly referred to as congestion pricing) launched on 5 January 2025. The zone covers all streets and avenues in Manhattan south of and including 60th Street. The base toll for passenger vehicles is USD 9, charged once per day, collected electronically.

On **27 December 2024**, the OFM issued **Diplomatic Note 24-2688**, making a formal determination under the Foreign Missions Act that foreign missions and international organisations subject to that determination are **exempt** from the Congestion Relief Zone charges. The note states that the exemption applies to charges that have been or will be assessed against any foreign mission or international organisation covered by the determination.

This distinguishes New York from London, where the UK government's position is that missions are liable for the Congestion Charge. In New York, the exemption is a formal federal determination, not a disputed policy position.

The exemption applies to vehicles of missions and international organisations. For specific questions about which vehicles and personnel are covered by the determination, contact the OFM New York office.

## Parking in Manhattan

Diplomatic plates provide certain parking privileges in New York City, but the position is more nuanced than it appears. While mission vehicles have traditionally been exempt from some parking enforcement actions by virtue of diplomatic immunity, the NYC Department of Finance has historically pursued outstanding Penalty Charge Notices against missions through diplomatic channels.

For practical purposes: observe posted parking restrictions where possible, particularly in midtown locations. Hydrant, bus stop, and no-stopping zones are consistently enforced. The areas around the UN complex and mission premises have specific local arrangements managed at the mission level.

## The registration procedure

All requests pass through your mission's administrative office, which submits to OFM New York. Documents typically required include confirmation of diplomatic status, vehicle purchase documentation, and proof of insurance. The OFM New York office handles titling and plate issuance.

For international organisation staff: the process is similar but coordinated through your organisation's administrative service to OFM New York.
